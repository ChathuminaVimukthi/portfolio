I'm Chathumina Vimukthi, a software engineer spending the last year figuring out what it means to actually be a Tech Lead. I work across backend systems and cloud infrastructure, though I'm increasingly spending time on the team and delivery side of things, which turned out to be a different kind of challenge than I expected.

## Engineering Background

I started as a backend developer, mostly in .NET and Azure. I liked the puzzle-solving side of the work — understanding how systems fit together, why something was slow, how to structure something so it didn't become a mess three months later. Over a few years I built out APIs, worked on data pipelines, and picked up enough cloud infrastructure to be dangerous with it. I was comfortable with the technical side. That was the job, and it made sense.

## The Shift to Tech Lead

The transition wasn't a decision I made so much as something that happened gradually. I started getting pulled into conversations that weren't purely technical — planning sessions, risk discussions, stakeholder updates. At some point I had to own a delivery, not just contribute to one. That changes how you think about the work.

The honest version is that I wasn't fully prepared for it. I knew the codebase well. I didn't know how to run a sprint effectively, how to communicate a delay without it feeling like a failure, or how to have a hard conversation with someone on the team without it getting awkward. Those things I had to learn on the job, and I'm still learning them.

## What I Own Now

Right now I'm leading a small team of engineers. I'm responsible for delivery, which means planning, unblocking, and making sure things actually ship. There's a client-facing element too: translating between what we're building and what stakeholders need to hear, which is its own skill. I still write code, though less than I used to. Most of my energy goes into roadmap planning, technical decisions that affect the whole team, and making sure people have what they need to do their best work.

## Where I'm Growing

The thing I'm most focused on right now is getting better at communicating decisions — especially the ones that involve tradeoffs or bad news. I can reason through a technical problem clearly, but translating that reasoning for a non-technical audience, or helping a senior stakeholder understand why the simple-looking option isn't actually simple, is still something I have to work at.

I'm also trying to get better at building an environment where people can raise problems early, admit uncertainty, and disagree without it feeling risky. That's called psychological safety and it's one of those things that sounds obvious in theory and is genuinely hard to build in practice. I think I'm making progress. I'm not sure I'd know if I wasn't.

This site is a place to document that process: what I'm learning, what I'm building, and occasionally what I got wrong. Not a resume. More like a working log.
